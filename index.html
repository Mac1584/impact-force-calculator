04 / READ THE RESULT

Why speed and mass aren't enough

Force depends on how quickly momentum changes. The same two camels could experience very different average forces if the collision lasts a different amount of time.

01

Contact time changes force

Impulse is the change in momentum. Divide it by contact time to estimate the average contact force during the full collision.

Favg ≈ impulse ÷ time
02

Compression gives another view

Relative motion energy divided by total compression estimates the average force during the stopping or compression phase. It does not include the full rebound phase.

Fcompression ≈ relative energy ÷ distance
03

Peak force remains unknown

Neither average reveals the instant of greatest force. You would need a force-time curve, material behavior, or measurements to estimate a peak reliably.

i

An educational model, not a safety rating

This calculator treats camels as point masses in a straight-line collision. Real impacts involve body shape, rotation, uneven contact, changing force, and complex tissue or material behavior. Default masses and collision conditions are illustrative; the 23.7 mph speed is a racing-speed example, not a universal camel average. Do not use these numbers to assess injury risk, design protective equipment, or make safety decisions.
